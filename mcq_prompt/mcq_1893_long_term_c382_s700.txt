You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Delia and Sarah are setting up a hidden play space behind the storage tables during morning free play.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Delia, Sarah, what are you two doing back here behind the storage tables with all those cushions?

STUDENT: (Whispering to Sarah while cuddling together on the cushions) We are playing the Gymnastics Game. Pretend I am the coolest girl in town.

TUTOR: I see. And how did the black wicker Teacher Chair get back here?

STUDENT: (Nervously glances around to see if anyone is watching and secretes the chair further under the table) Sarah brought it. We need an exclusive space.

TUTOR: Well, Lizzie is walking over here now to see what you are playing. What should we say to her?

STUDENT: I’m so sorry, Lizzie, but only two are allowed in Gymnastics. The space is not big enough for three. I can tell you are feeling sad. Try again later.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

D: Maintain a 'leaky, viscous, orientation to control' by letting the games go on and giving the children time to develop their mischief before finally breaking up the escalating unruliness.
