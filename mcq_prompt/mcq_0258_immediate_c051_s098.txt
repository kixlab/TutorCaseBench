You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Evie is in the lounge at 7:30am eating Weetabix for breakfast while watching television.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Good morning Evie, it's 7:30am. Here is your Weetabix for breakfast.

STUDENT: (Evie sits watching television with a mouthful of Weetabix, which she is not chewing.)

TUTOR: Keep eating, please.

STUDENT: (After holding the food in her mouth for several minutes, she starts pushing the Weetabix out so that it falls onto her chin. She looks towards the tutor, holds eye contact, makes short vocalizations, and struggles to wipe her face.)

TUTOR: Let's try to eat a little more. Here comes the spoon with some Weetabix.

STUDENT: (Evie turns her head away.)

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Based on the dialogue above, which intervention is most appropriate for this student in this situation?

C: The tutor helps Evie to reflect on the consequences of her actions and on the emotions and states of mind of the other people involved in the lounge (for example, saying, 'They are sad because...').
D: The tutor explicitly states to Evie that mistakes during breakfast do not matter. The tutor models this mindset by admitting to making mistakes themselves despite their expertise, discussing this concept with her in the lounge.
