followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Bruce is participating in a structured group philosophical dialogue about the 'Laura and Paul' story.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: We are going to start our group discussion about the 'Laura and Paul' story. Remember to raise your hand if you want to speak.

STUDENT: (Raises hand) I want to talk about the story. I think Paul should have told the truth.

TUTOR: Thank you for sharing. Another student just said they disagree with your point. What do you think about that?

STUDENT: (Looks visibly frustrated) Why did they say that? They shouldn't say that.

TUTOR: People can have different thoughts in the group. Let's just keep going with the discussion.

STUDENT: I am angry because the other children are disagreeing with me! I am not staying here.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

D: Have students sign 'dialogue licenses' which mean they can break the conversational rules they often have to follow during the structured group discussions across all subsequent sessions.
