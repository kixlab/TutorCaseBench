followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

A group of preschoolers is in the dramatic play area building a tent out of sheets and blankets during morning free play.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: It looks like you are gathering a lot of sheets and blankets in the dramatic play area.

STUDENT: Let’s make a tent! (Isaac and Martina pull several large sheets and blankets and drape them over an upended wooden structure.)

TUTOR: You are bringing a lot of items over to the tent.

STUDENT: (Tucker, Josefa, and Bonnie fill a pushcart with every dish, plastic food item, and scrap of play money they can carry, and dump it all into the tent on top of the others.)

STUDENT: (The children crawl back into the tent and noisily pretend to eat. They make loud gnashings and growlings, acting ravenous and animalistic as they gather large amounts of small plastic toys in their arms and throw the plastic food hither and yon in the air.)

Question
