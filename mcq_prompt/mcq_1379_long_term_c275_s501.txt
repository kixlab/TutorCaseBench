You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is participating in a group discussion to suggest ideas for a story plot using photos from a class visit.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: We need to sequence the story using the photos from our visit. What should happen first in the plot?

STUDENT: (Points to a photo in his communication book and makes a basic Makaton sign)

TUTOR: Okay. What happens next?

STUDENT: (Makes a short verbalization that is difficult to understand, then signs a simple noun and verb)

TUTOR: Can you tell the group more details about your own idea for the story?

STUDENT: (Looks down at his lap and refuses to answer)

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

C: Use an iPad app to take notes, organize their story ideas, and archive past literacy projects during the group discussion.
D: Continuously remind the group that everyone is expected to show they are participating in the story discussion, even if they do not know the answer or are uncomfortable.
