You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The teacher is checking a daily homework assignment on paragraph topics with the whole class while Helen sits near a peer named Wes.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Let's go now to Part A. Topics of paragraphs. This is homework, so everybody is ready. In Part A we have six paragraphs. 'A five tastes of food' is in paragraph three. (Looking at the book)

STUDENT: (Employs a quick flick check at the teacher, hops up from her chair and leans over the table, dragging Wes's book closer to her, then gets her cell phone from Wes and smiles.)

TUTOR: How about b... Our five senses? Lisa, Helen. (Walking toward the board while looking back down at the book)

STUDENT: (Glancing at Wes, then to the teacher, then to her book) One. (Her cell phone rings, and she picks it up and laughs)

TUTOR: Very good. Thank you. Yes, paragraph one. Helen, you get the point. (Standing with back facing towards Helen, eyes looking at the book, making a chart on the board)

STUDENT: (Picks up an object from the table and throws it at Wes, still holding her cell phone, looking at it and laughing. Then picks up a bigger object from the table and throws it at Wes.)

Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

D: Agree with the student that she is having a rough time with the homework assignment, validating her feelings during the activity.
