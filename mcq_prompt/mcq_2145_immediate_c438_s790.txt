You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is engaging in socio-dramatic free play with female peers in the dark room and loft area using a mirror and shells.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: What are you girls doing in the dark room with those shells?

STUDENT: (Fixing her hair in the mirror) We're playing sassy girls! They be mean, and selfish, they hurt people. (Gives a loud mocking laugh)

TUTOR: What else do sassy girls do?

STUDENT: And we're naughty people. Ya ... we sneak up on people in their houses, and take all the people the people like the best away. We'll make Luke eat poison. He will eat it and die. We will trick him.

TUTOR: I will play with you, but I don't want to be mean.

STUDENT: They're mean. You can play with us, but you have to be mean! If you're not going to be mean, you can't play with us. (Sticks out her tongue and spits)

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

C: Deliver verbal reinforcement when a replacement behavior occurs instead of the aggressive "sassy girl" actions during the free play.
D: Maintain an absence of direct intervention by stepping back and observing the socio-dramatic free play without interfering in the activity.
