Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is at a gymnastics workshop station in a PE class, using an instruction card to practice somersaults and jumps with a small group.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Please look at the instruction cards at this station and start practicing your somersaults and jumps. Make sure to manage your repetitions.

STUDENT: Here, I arrive at the activity, quickly read the sheet to go through the various exercises. Here, I want to check if the exercises are hard. (Executes them one by one, slowly and precisely)

TUTOR: Keep practicing the exercises on the sheet.

STUDENT: Well, I try my best and then I practice this somersault, the hardest! (Makes a correct jump, puts up the equipment, and waits idly)

TUTOR: It is time for your next repetition.

STUDENT: (Chats with friends, gives a kick to a mat, and begins wandering about the gym) I work a bit but in the end it's boring to do the same thing over and over again, so I have a laugh with my mates. It's no big deal, and after that I work as well.

D: Ask the student to describe the physical form of the somersault they just performed at the station.
