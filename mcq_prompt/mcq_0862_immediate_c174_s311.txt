You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student approaches the instructor's desk at the end of the lesson to hand over a physical copy of a complaint letter.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Class is dismissed. Shelli, I see you are waiting at my desk. Do you need something?

STUDENT: (Approaches the desk and hands over a physical copy of a letter) I am giving this to you now because I could not find your box.

TUTOR: Thank you. What is this letter regarding?

STUDENT: It is the same letter I published on the course’s internet forum. Today’s lesson was no different from previous lessons. I want to protest against the level, the conduct of the teacher and the way the material is taught.

TUTOR: I understand. I will review what you have submitted.

STUDENT: (Speaking loudly as a few students stay behind and stare curiously) The course is conducted in a theatrical and joking manner, what I would call worthless psychology. I have also sent the same complaint letter to the head of the program.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Based on the dialogue above, which intervention is most appropriate for this student in this situation?

A: Explain calmly that a complaint letter should be submitted to the instructor on the same day, not a month later, and ask how she dared express her thoughts on a forum of 80 students without trying to talk beforehand.
B: Explicitly request clarification or further examples regarding the specific claims in her physical complaint letter to push the dialogue philosophically while the other students are still watching.
C: Guide the student to use a 'bubble-blowing' technique right at the desk to visualize her thoughts about the course and release them without judgment before discussing the physical complaint letter.
D: Probe beneath the student's loud responses at the desk, encouraging her to think at a deeper level and helping her perceive a range of perspectives about the course and the situation.
