SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is in a social and health care training classroom being asked to read facts about human physiology and diabetes to prepare for a test.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Today we are going to learn the facts about human physiology and diabetes to prepare for your tests. Please sit at your desks and read the first chapter on how the body processes sugar.

STUDENT: Do we really have to just sit and read this? I thought we were going to use that box of insulin pens and injection needles you have on the desk.

TUTOR: We need to cover the theory first. Please focus on the text in front of you.

STUDENT: But this just feels like irrelevant information. My grandmother suffers from diabetes, and she just had to change her diet. I don't see why we need to memorize all these biology terms.

TUTOR: You need to know this for when you administer insulin later. Keep trying to read through it.

STUDENT: (Pulls out cell phone) I have a hard time just to sit and take in all this reading when there's no clear explicit connection to the practical work. I'm just going to take photos of these different coloured pens instead.
