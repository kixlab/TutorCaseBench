Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Bill is participating in a practice test at a table in the back corner of the room.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: I am distributing the practice test booklets now. Please get ready to begin.

STUDENT: (Pulls his chair into the corner at the back of the room between a table and a cabinet, readjusting his table to have it touch his belly so he sits tall and moves nothing but his feet.) Can I take my test back here?

TUTOR: Yes, you can sit there. Let's get started.

STUDENT: (Sits still, looks silently at the teacher distributing the test booklets, and then directs his gaze on his own booklet.)

TUTOR: Keep working on your test.

STUDENT: (Stretches his arms away from his body so that he touches the opposite end of his table, but soon starts to bend over to check the content of some baskets next to him.)

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────
