You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is participating in a Lunchtime Science group discussion and reviewing a case study about prenatal health and the environment.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Please read this case study about prenatal health and the environment and share your thoughts with the group.

STUDENT: (Speaking loudly over the group) Why do we have to talk about this? I'm not wanting to be a mom, so this has nothing to do with me.

TUTOR: Let's just focus on the reading. What do you think about the article?

STUDENT: I mean, I was at the store yesterday and my friend was yelling about these new shoes she bought. It was crazy.

TUTOR: Can you try to connect that story to the science topic we are discussing?

STUDENT: I don't know how it connects. Like I said, my friends and I are not nature people. This whole science thing just doesn't give me any new material or ways to interact with people.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

C: Revoices and tosses back the student's statements to the larger group, suggests uncertainty about the tutor's own knowledge, shares resources about the topic, and questions the student's sources of evidence.
D: Shows a short film that depicts others in need of help to elicit compassion from the student regarding the prenatal health case study during the session.
