You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is writing a weekly journal entry reflecting on her group's progress in discussing interview questions and interviewee selection for their research project.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: For this week's journal, please reflect on your group's progress in discussing interview questions and interviewee selection for the research project.

STUDENT: I have some mean and terrible thoughts about Charles. Because this is a journal entry, I feel the freedom to express these thoughts.

TUTOR: Please explain what happened during your group meeting.

STUDENT: When we were discussing interview questions Charles contributed that he felt there was no racial diversity on our campus and depending on the age and race of the student we interviewed, they would have different answers. He got really loud and rambled on in an increasingly dominant voice about how he was Hispanic and older.

TUTOR: How did you respond to his comments?

STUDENT: On the discussion boards he talks about how he wants team cohesion so we can work better together yet he continually mentions his race and age. I am just disregarding his words. I don't understand this, we're all different and come from different backgrounds. Race shouldn't matter and we should see past it.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Based on the dialogue above, which intervention is most appropriate for this student in this situation?

A: Produce authoritativeness in the journal response by writing in short sentences, like headlines in a newspaper, mentioning only the main details briefly and concisely.
B: Provide active tutor intervention in the group discussion board to directly facilitate the group dynamics and manage the students' oral participation regarding the interview questions.
C: Begin with tutor explanation and modeling of the journal reflection, followed by a gradual release of responsibility to the student for analyzing the group interaction.
D: Respond privately to the student in her journal, challenging her to consider how race matters and to sociologically understand her interactions and emotions concerning Charles.
