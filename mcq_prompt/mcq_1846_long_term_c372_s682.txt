You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is sitting at an isolated desk in a classroom, expected to complete a math worksheet from a textbook.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Please sit at your desks, open your math books, and complete the equations on the worksheet.

STUDENT: I have a hard time just sitting here and taking this in. Math is just irrelevant information for what I actually want to do.

TUTOR: This is part of the general subjects curriculum. Please try to focus on the paper.

STUDENT: I have strong negative associations with these general subjects. I want to do actual work-based activities with concrete materials, not just stare at a book.

TUTOR: We need to finish this math assignment today. Just do your best to get through it.

STUDENT: (Stands up and pushes the books and papers off the desk) I'm not doing this traditional stuff anymore! It's completely useless! Hey, who wants to play cards instead of doing this stupid math?

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

C: Review previously identified consequence maps before the math session. Give the student the clipboard with the maps and calm-down schedule, let them decide which to listen to first, then read the negative behaviors and consequences first, followed by the positive.
D: Reform the duration of the general subjects course to forty weeks, spend more classes in the workshops, and use classes with a smaller number of students and a set team of teachers.
