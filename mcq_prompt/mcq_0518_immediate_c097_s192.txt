You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is participating in a whole-class verbal discussion about a stimulus passage while sitting in a circle.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: We are going to talk about the stimulus passage we just read. Please share your thoughts with the circle.

STUDENT: I think the story is... well, I want to make a point about the main character, but... (looks over at a classmate who is whispering).

TUTOR: What do you want to say about the character?

STUDENT: He was wrong. I mean, I find it easier to get on with people when I use philosophy and just the agreeing and disagreeing in general, but I don't know how to structure what I want to say right now.

TUTOR: Just go ahead and tell us why you think he was wrong.

STUDENT: I can't think when they keep trying to distract me! I don't have my support assistant here to help me stay on task. I can't cope with this right now, I'm removing myself from the session.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

C: Require participants in the circle to raise a hand and wait to be called upon to speak, without necessarily calling on them in order.
D: Project the stimulus passage from slides onto a big screen in the classroom, adding multimedia elements like sound effects and motion features.
