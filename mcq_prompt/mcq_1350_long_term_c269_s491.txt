You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Idit is in a one-on-one interview using circle drawings to explain whether 2/9 or 3/4 is bigger.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Let's look at these fractions. Can you draw circles to show 2/9 and 3/4 and tell me which one is bigger?

STUDENT: And then if I have, uh... (draws circle with four small circles in it) four... and I draw um three (colors three of the four circles)...

TUTOR: Okay, what do you think?

STUDENT: So um... (raises eyebrows while looking at her drawing, tilts her head) my opinion is that this is bigger (points to the 2/9 drawing).

TUTOR: Why? How is it derived from this drawing?

STUDENT: (Continues looking at the drawing, tilts her head), um-hm... (chuckles, then sits up, mumbling) I don't know, that's how I thought of it... (Smiling, mumbles) I don't know, there are things I can't do.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

D: Use a visual schedule that identifies the activities of the interview and the reinforcers the student is eligible to receive for contingent performance.
