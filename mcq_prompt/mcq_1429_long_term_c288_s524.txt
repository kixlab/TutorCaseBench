Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is sitting in a 12th grade expository writing class and is asked to begin their first writing assignment.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Today we are starting our first writing assignment for this 12th grade expository writing class. Please look at the prompt and begin your essay.

STUDENT: In my opinion I don’t feel writing is important. If I want to be a doctor I’m not going to write a five paragraph essay on why their ankle is broken. I will just scribble some notes and still get paid. Writing is only important when you want to look like you know what you’re talking about or want to act smart.

TUTOR: It is still a requirement for this class. Why don't you just try to get started on the first paragraph?

STUDENT: Writing is never going to be easy for me. I never know what to write about or how to start it. I’m not good at spelling and I think that I would rather just talk to people. Writing is just kinda dumb.

TUTOR: You just need to put some effort into it. Keep trying and the words will come to you.

STUDENT: (Pushes the blank assignment away) I’ve never passed an English class in my life. And don’t plan on it now. I’m Mexican so how am I really supposed to be good at this stuff? I'm not doing it.
