You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is participating in a Marketing Management II seminar and is expected to share their reading analysis with their diverse project group.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: It is time to work on the Marketing Management II group project. Please go ahead and share your ideas on the readings with your group members.

STUDENT: Could you look at my work first? With my assignments, I still try to receive the instructor’s feedback; so when I share my work with others, I have the right answers.

TUTOR: The goal of the seminar is to discuss the readings together. You should just share what you have with them now.

STUDENT: I feel a sense of responsibility to act as a model student so that other members do not look down upon Korean students. I am very conscious of their evaluation of my performance.

TUTOR: Just try to convince them to accept your ideas. What did they say when you started the discussion?

STUDENT: With peers who are stubborn and defensive, I rarely say anything because I don’t want to bring negativity to the group. I just lost my self-confidence and withdrew from group participation before even attempting to convince others.

Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

C: Inform students their reading analyses will not be graded for content and their identities will be protected through pseudonyms and edited comments.
D: Incorporate group projects that account for a large proportion of the final grade, requiring several hours of weekly peer interaction.
