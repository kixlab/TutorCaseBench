Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is attempting a reading comprehension task using a Braille book in a bustling school environment.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Please open your Braille book and read the first page so we can answer the comprehension questions.

STUDENT: (Runs hands over the edge of the book) The spiral binding on this book is distracting. And the paper makes too much sound when I turn it. I prefer audio books, because it is faster and easier to understand when you do not have to engage in tactile reading.

TUTOR: We need to use the paper book today. Go ahead and start reading.

STUDENT: It's too noisy in the hallway. Nobody should shout, but speak in a low tone of voice and be quiet if the teacher says so. I don’t understand why students don’t do as the teacher says.

TUTOR: Just try to ignore the noise and focus on understanding the story.

STUDENT: I can't concentrate on understanding what I am reading. The feel of certain letters is distracting, and with all this noise... If it’s not calm, sensible and quiet, I just cannot think!

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

B: Provide the student with praise for specific desirable behaviors at least once every two minutes during the reading task.
C: Issue the sanction of a verbal warning to the student regarding their complaints about the reading task and the noise.
D: Terminate the reading demands and remove the Braille book and other materials from the student for ten seconds.
