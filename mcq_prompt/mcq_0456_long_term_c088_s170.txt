You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is sitting on the rug with a group for a read-aloud of the book 'Visiting Day'.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Alright everyone, please sit in your designated spots on the rug. Today we are going to do a read-aloud of the book 'Visiting Day'.

STUDENT: (Looking at another student instead of sitting in his spot) Ooh, ooh! I knew it! The father is in jail and little girl’s visiting her father.

TUTOR: Please remember our rules for the rug. You need to sit quietly in your spot and not talk out of turn.

STUDENT: But you know, there are so many rules to remember that I can’t remember what I was gonna say sometimes.

TUTOR: We just need to sit still and be quiet so I can start reading the story to the group.

STUDENT: (Sighs heavily) I’ve already heard it four times! (Slumps over, turns his back away, and averts his gaze)

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

C: Continuously remind the student that they are expected to show they are participating during the read-aloud, even if they are not completely comfortable.
D: Give attention to the student's avoidance strategies as compensations rather than refusals to comply with the expected task of sitting quietly during the read-aloud.
