You are an expert special education evaluator assessing tutor responses to students with learning difficulties.

Below is a tutoring dialogue between a tutor and a student with learning difficulties,
followed by a multiple-choice question about the most appropriate intervention.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

Jesus is reading his choice book, Monster, during sustained silent reading time in English class.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: It is time for sustained silent reading. Please take out your choice book, Monster, and read quietly.

STUDENT: Okay. I picked this one because the story seemed realistic. I used to read pretty slow and not understand the words but now I’m a lot better.

TUTOR: That is good to hear. Please begin reading.

STUDENT: I'm trying, but the words are hard to understand in this part.

TUTOR: Just keep trying your best to read it.

STUDENT: (Puts the book down and turns to watch the other students in the class) I can't focus when these words are so hard. It just makes reading unappealing, and I'd rather see what they are doing.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question
──────────────────────────────────────────────

B: Issue the sanction of a verbal warning regarding the student's off-task behavior during the reading time.
C: Withhold attention from the student when they engage in the problem behavior of putting the book down.
D: Model how one reads and learns through teaching strategies in short time segments utilizing a variety of contexts.
