SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is reading a newspaper article about Aboriginal gangs in an alternative education classroom to analyze the author's tone and language.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Let's look at the newspaper article about Aboriginal gangs again so we can finish talking about the author's tone and the language they used.

STUDENT: Fair? How can it be fair? They watch us like hawks, like criminals, and not the other kids at break. How come white guys are allowed to walk inside the shop? They can do anything. Nobody is following, and we have the stupid man near us all the time.

TUTOR: Let's try to focus on the text. Please read the next section on your own so we can look at the audience.

STUDENT: Boring...boring...boring. It’s all stupid. I didn’t come to school to learn this.

TUTOR: We just need to finish what we started yesterday with the article.

STUDENT: (Pushes the newspaper article away) I'm not reading it again. You want to know about gangs? Let's talk about gang clothing, weapons, and initiation rites. That's how gang protection and hierarchies actually work.
