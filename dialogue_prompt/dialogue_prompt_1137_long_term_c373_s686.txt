──────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENARIO
──────────────────────────────────────────────

The student is working independently on three preliminary reading comprehension questions about a text titled 'Animals in the Home' during a 4th grade language arts lesson.

──────────────────────────────────────────────
DIALOGUE
──────────────────────────────────────────────

TUTOR: Please begin answering the three preliminary reading comprehension questions about the text 'Animals in the Home' on your worksheet.

STUDENT: (Pauses for about half a minute before starting to write. She holds the text sheet in her hands under the worksheet and shifts her attention back and forth from one sheet to the other. For the first question, she copies the details exactly but also records superfluous information, including the issue number and both the Hebrew and Gregorian dates of publication.) Only to write the answer.

TUTOR: Okay, keep going with the rest of the worksheet.

STUDENT: (Looks at the instructions for the second question for about half a minute, sits for another minute facing the text sheet without doing anything, and then moves to read the third question.) I went on to do the third question because I saw this was too difficult for me and so that I would not waste time.

TUTOR: Make sure you go back and try to answer the second question: 'What can be learned from these details?' What do you think?

STUDENT: (Returns to the second question. She looks at the assignment sheet while holding her head between her hands, reads the text, returns to the assignment sheet, wipes her nose, picks up her pencil, and puts it down. She sits for a relatively long time without asking for help.) I tried to find out the answer… I didn’t understand the question.
